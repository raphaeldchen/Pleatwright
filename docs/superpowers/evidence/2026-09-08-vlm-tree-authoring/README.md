`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-07-vlm-metric-tree-authoring-design.md`.
Preserved because the spec asserts measured numbers and, until this commit,
**every artifact supporting them lived in a session temp directory.**

`scripts/` is **as-run, not productionised.** These are probes in the sense of
`probe_mesh_bakeoff.py` — they were written to answer a question once, they
hard-code absolute paths from the session that ran them, and they are kept for
reproducibility and audit, not for reuse. Do not import them.

## ⚠ Read the saturation caveat before citing any null here

Across all six experimental arms the non-exact cells are
`{A,B,C,none,text,visual}/spider` — **the spider, and only ever the spider.**
Eight of nine subjects are exact in every arm, so one cell can move. Every null
above means *"this corpus cannot distinguish these conditions"*, not *"this
intervention does not work"*. That is why spec §S7 (a metric with headroom)
gates all further work.


## 2026-09-09 — the corpus was TWO prompt configurations, and one tree was upside down

`run17_raw.json` predates commit 9209d07, which put the two-frames warning into
`loop_lib.SYSTEM`. Only `dog` and `rabbit` were re-run against it, so the
committed 17 were a mixture. Reading the sheets to build a review page found a
third inverted tree, the **dolphin**: its readings were right (dorsal base
v=7, tip v=1, matching the mesh) and every vertical component of its emitted
directions was sign-flipped. It scored EXACT on leaf count, clean on the
contract, and 0.28 on reading fidelity. **No metric in this repo saw it.**

`run_framefix15.py` re-authored the other fifteen against the corrected prompt.
The uniform corpus is `frame_fix_raw.json` (dog, rabbit) + `frame_fix15_raw.json`
(the rest); score it with `score_uniform17.py`. **`run17_raw.json` is kept only
so the before/after is auditable — do not quote it as a result.**

Measured under one identical, corrected metric:

|---|---|---|
| MAE | 0.29 | 0.29 |
| leaf count exact | 13/17 | 13/17 |

**The re-run bought orientation and uniformity, and nothing else.** Anatomy
accuracy did not move; packing moved one cell in the losing direction, which at
a measured MAE noise floor of 0.059 is not a result. The composition of the
misses did change — the ant lost its antennae and became exact, the crab
gained a `rostrum` and stopped being — so per-subject stability is weaker than
the aggregate suggests.

Two metric defects were found by this run and fixed, both of which had made
CORRECT trees look wrong:

* `reading_fidelity.parse_readings` required bare `(u,v)` pairs, so the bat's
  `(24, z=9.5)` and the dolphin's `(x=8.5,y=0.5)` scored as **no readings at
  all** — indistinguishable from total protocol non-compliance.
* `leaf_label_accuracy` did not know `pect_left` (abbreviation) or `stinger`
  (the telson ending the metasoma), understating coverage to 0.500 and 0.889.
  Closing that exposed the opposite hole: `pincer_L`/`pincer_R` matched nothing,
  dropped out of the comparison, and let the scorpion score EXACT at 11 leaves
  against an expected 9. `exact` now requires every leaf to be recognised.

**No PNG in this run is committed; every picture is derivable from the JSON.**
`scripts/gridtest/` (the arm-C renders the model reads) is 4.4 MB — rebuild it

## Traps this run walked into, all of which cost a run

* `LLM_MAX_TOKENS` defaults to **4096** — truncates the tool call in 24 of 27
  cells (`finish_reason: "length"`). A uniform failure across all arms **looks
  like a null result**; the tell was that the only survivor was the smallest
  tree, not the clearest picture.
* A bare `asyncio.run(main())` with no `__main__` guard meant importing the
  runner for one helper silently re-ran the entire 27-cell sweep.
* Persisting only at the end lost 14 completed, billed cells when a run stopped
  early. Write after every cell.
* `tools: []` is a 400 on this endpoint; `run_turn` guards `tool_choice` for the
  tool-less case but not `tools`.
* `APIConnectionError` is transient, a credit-exhaustion 400 is terminal, and
  both arrive as exceptions. **Read the message, not the type.**

**What actually happened.** The two rolls that reached the repair turn offered
no `head` candidate (spider #2 `candidates=["abdomen"]`, crab #1
`candidates=[]`); for the other six, the flap counts (spider 9, scorpion 11,
crab 10 — each one below the 2026-09-12 baseline of 10/12/11) are consistent
with the head no longer being authored, but leaf names were not recorded by
this run, so that is not a claim this data backs — `leaf_names` is now
recorded for the next one (driver fix below). That is the rule-4 carve-out
from Task 8 moving the flap count down before the repair turn ever runs, not
the merge turn doing anything. Both
spider and scorpion mostly *packed outright* (spider #0/#1 at `flatten`;
scorpion #0/#1/#2 at `order`, `none` (exact, attempt 0), and `placement`) —
zero repair calls needed, because the carve-out alone removed enough flaps to
clear the wall. Only two of nine rolls reached the ladder-exhaustion / merge
path at all, and neither produced a merge:

- **spider #2** exhausted all 55 ladder rungs (`placement` 2, `order` 5,
  `flatten` 6, `jitter` 24, `composed` 10, `stub` 8), reached the repair turn
  with exactly one unpaired candidate (`abdomen`), and the model **declined**
  it, verbatim: "The abdomen is a clearly protruding, distinct body mass
  behind the cephalothorax (visible as a separate bulb in the render), not a
  fused stub, so it is a real appendage and cannot be merged; with no other
  unpaired flaps available, none qualifies." The original tree went to the
  gate and does not pack.
- **crab #1** exhausted 47 rungs (`order` 5, `placement` 2, `flatten` 6,
  `jitter` 24, `composed` 10) and the repair turn **skipped without a model
  call**, verbatim: "no unpaired flap: every flap is one of a bilateral pair."
  No candidates existed, so no anatomy-spending decision was ever put to the
  model, and no tokens were spent asking.
- **crab #0** never reached the packer or the repair machinery at all —
  authoring itself failed contract retries (`unbuildable: 'nodes'`), so
  `status` is `failed` and `outcome` is now `authoring_failed` (see driver fix
  below), not `packed`.

So of the two rolls that fired the last-resort turn, one ended in a decline
and one in a no-candidates skip — zero merges happened in this run. `rungs_tried`
was ≥ 1 (55 and 47) on both rows where the repair path ran, and `None` on
every row that packed outright or failed before packing — the last-resort
property held: the model is never consulted before the ladder is exhausted,
and never consulted when the tree already packs.

Taken together: the loop's gates were all exercised in this run — ladder
exhaustion, candidate enumeration, a real model call, and a decline all fired
at least once — and the safeguards held (a bilaterally-paired flap was never
offered as a candidate; the model refused to spend real anatomy when only one
candidate existed). But because zero merges occurred, **whether a merge
actually rescues a wall subject remains unmeasured by this run.**

**Driver fix.** `_row`'s `outcome` field previously read
`r.get("outcome") if r else "packed"`, which mislabelled crab #0 — an
authoring failure that never reached the packer or the repair record — as
`outcome=packed`. Fixed to: `"packed"` when `packing.served`;
`"authoring_failed"` when `status == "failed"`; the repair record's own
`outcome` when a repair record exists; else `None`. The committed JSON's
`outcome` column was re-derived under the same rule without re-running the
live driver (it costs real API spend); only crab #0 changed, from `packed` to
`authoring_failed` — every other row's `outcome` was already correct.

A second, review-caught defect in that same precedence: because the served
check ran before the repair-record check, a roll that merged a flap AND THEN
packed would have read `outcome="packed"`, silently dropping the fact that a
merge happened even though `merged_flap`/`removed_nodes` were still recorded
correctly. Fixed to check the repair record's own `outcome == "merged"`
first, ahead of `packing.served` — the `packed`/`stage` columns already carry
the second pack's result, so `"merged"` says everything `"packed"` did, plus
the merge. No committed row is affected by this fix: zero merges occurred in
this run (see above), so no row's `outcome` was ever `"merged"` to begin
with. The driver also now records a `leaf_names` column
(`TA.leaves_of(...)` on the authored tree) so a future run's "no `head`
candidate" claims are backed by data instead of inferred from flap counts;
the nine rows above predate that column and carry no `leaf_names` key.

**What remains unmeasured / follow-ups the data points at:**
1. A real merge — every subject either packed before reaching the repair turn
   or reached it with a candidate set that led to a decline or a skip. The
   merge-and-recompile path (`apply_merge`, `after_flaps`) is exercised by
   unit tests but not by this live run.
2. Authoring robustness — crab #0's `unbuildable: 'nodes'` failure is the same
   failure class as the ant's on the 2026-09-12 (baseline) corpus run; it is a
   recurring authoring-contract fragility, not specific to this slice.
3. Flap count is not the only wall variable — spider #2 still exhausted the
   full 55-rung ladder at 9 flaps (one fewer than the 10-flap baseline that
   established the wall), so removing one flap via the rule-4 carve-out does
   not by itself guarantee the ladder clears.

## 2026-09-13 — review of the full17 trees; bat #2 regenerated

Reviewing the 51 served trees against the saved `state.json` files (not the pictures) gave
four findings worth keeping. Data: `data/repair_rolls_bat_redo.json`,
`scripts/repair_rolls_out_bat_redo/` (untracked, 3 rolls).

- **Directions never reach the compiler.** `metric_tree` carries `{id, parent_id, length}`
  only; the per-node `direction` exists to draw the four-view sheet. Horse #1 and frog #1
  (hind legs crossing the front legs), elephant #1 (V-shaped spine, root below the back line)
  and bat #2 (rotated 90°) are all drawing-side errors: the crease pattern is unchanged.
- **Joints DO reach the compiler, and they cost.** Degree-2 nodes are passed through, and
  TreeMaker emits a hinge crease at every tree node (`tmPoly.cpp:1088`). All seven rolls
