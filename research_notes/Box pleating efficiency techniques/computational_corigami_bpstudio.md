## Q1. COrigami (arXiv:2606.26299): stage formulation, packing efficiency, grid choice, gadgets used/omitted, metrics, limitations

### Takeaway
COrigami packs on a strict orthogonal integer grid with no Pythagorean stretches, level shifters, elevation changes or 22.5° gadgets. It says outright that it trades paper efficiency for success rate and recognisability. Grid size starts at a circle-packing-derived lower bound and goes up one unit at a time until a contiguous tiling exists; the final layout is picked by VLM score, not by grid size. The paper reports no efficiency metric (no scale, area utilisation or grid-vs-optimum ratio), only per-stage pass rates (packing 55.3%, solving 79.2%). It names "Pythagorean stretches or level shifters" in the backtracking packer as the main way to improve efficiency in future work.

**Pipeline overview**
- Stages: (1) Gemini writes a semantic stick figure; (2) "our backtracking solver converts this stick figure into a discrete 2D rectangle packing"; (3) a "hinge algorithm" yields a "guaranteed flat-foldable base crease pattern"; (4) "tree shaping" with simple folds, then (5) RL-finetuned Gemini orchestrates further shaping tools (narrowing); (6) a geometric folding simulator renders views for VLM reward — [COrigami §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Claimed novelty: "while expert human designers manually pack and solve box-pleated bases, no software previously existed to fully automate this contiguous tiling and flat-foldability process" — [COrigami §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Stick figure: each stick has a label, a scalar length, an azimuth and an elevation (3D direction angles, not BP "elevation"). Leaves become flaps and internal edges become rivers — [COrigami §3.1, App. C.1 example](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1). For packing and scoring, degree-2 chains are merged into single sticks — [COrigami App. C.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)

**Packing formulation (§3.2)**
- The packing is "a discrete rectangle-packing and tiling problem on a square integer grid". "Leaf nodes … are instantiated as rectangles whose dimensions are derived from the corresponding stick lengths"; "internal edges become paths of proportional width representing structural rivers" — [COrigami §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Rivers partition the grid into pockets. "Components sharing a joint in the tree must belong to the same pocket" — [COrigami §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Search: "iterative backtracking search over river placements and flap positions". Rivers are placed in a "traversal plan" order. The first river comes from exhaustive enumeration of straight full-width/height rivers or L-shaped rivers with one 90° turn. Each later river is placed by "a wall-following algorithm that traces the contour of previously placed elements, producing free-form paths that snake around existing obstacles". Each newly enclosed pocket is packed immediately. Flap positions are generated analytically (sliding along adjacency-constrained neighbours) or by brute-force grid enumeration, filtered by symmetry, overlap and area-feasibility checks, and ranked by a scoring heuristic — [COrigami §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Tiling by flap expansion: every unoccupied cell must be covered, because "a perfect tiling" is "a mathematical prerequisite for generating a physically viable base crease pattern". A backtracking search chooses a consistent set of flap enlargements — [COrigami §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- After tiling, diagonal ridges ("the straight skeleton of each region") partition the paper, and region boundaries give the hinges. The paper asserts this "achiev[es] an optimal use of the paper by minimizing the required grid size for a given stick figure". That optimality is only relative to its own incremental grid sweep; no proof or comparison is given — [COrigami §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Appendix E has one subsection, E.1 (grid initialisation). The wall-follower, scoring heuristic and traversal plan are not specified further — [COrigami App. E](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1); internal confirmation: "App. E holds only E.1 … Branching hubs, hub pairs, elevation and the scoring rule are not described" — internal `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-branching-hub-packer-design.md` (prior-art table)

**Grid-size choice (App. E.1, §4.2)**
- G_init = max(⌈√(Σ a_i)⌉, D), where D is the tree diameter (longest stick-length-weighted path). Area terms: the four longest flaps count a_i = l_i² ("packed as quarter circles"); a river whose neighbours are all flaps counts max(k_i·m_i, k_i²), with m_i the longest attached flap; a river with a non-flap neighbour counts k_i²; every other stick (e.g. a fifth or later flap) counts 2·l_i². For symmetric figures an odd G_init is rounded up to even — [COrigami App. E.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- The sweep "increments this base resolution in unit steps, re-attempting packing at each successive grid size until a valid tiling is found or a maximum bound is reached". Packing variety also comes from "sweeping across different grid resolutions" and from varying solver hyperparameters and hinge assignments — [COrigami §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- The final layout is not chosen by efficiency: "rather than selecting the final layout based on mathematical scale or area utilization, the system defers to the Vision-Language Model … [to] filter out visually poor or overly bulky layouts" — [COrigami App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Explicit trade-off: "Unlike traditional computational tools such as TreeMaker and BP Studio, which explicitly optimize for packing efficiency, COrigami optimizes for success rate and visual recognizability at the cost of efficiency" — [COrigami App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Lower anchor: naive "stacking" (flaps stacked vertically at full grid width, contour-hugging rivers; Lang's TreeMaker manual calls this Kawahata's "string-of-beads") "empirically packed 100% of our stick figures", but "often result[s] in grid sizes well over 100 even for simple models, which leads to prohibitively dense physical folds" — [COrigami App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)

**Gadgets and techniques used vs. omitted**
- Used: orthogonal axial pleats, 45° ridges (straight skeleton of each region) and hinges. Assignment uses interleaved (M-V-M-V) or "symmetric" (M-V-V-M) hinge patterns — [COrigami §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Omitted, as future work: "future iterations should incorporate more advanced structural routing mechanisms, such as Pythagorean stretches or level shifters, which human experts frequently utilize to achieve superior packing efficiencies on the grid. While these non-orthogonal elements have historically been restricted to interactive editors because they complicate automated tiling … integrating them into our backtracking packer would represent a significant step forward" — [COrigami §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- 22.5°: never mentioned as a technique. Hex pleating (30°) and the "countable infinity" of finite-propagation grids (Lang & Alperin 2014, "Graph paper for polygon-packed origami design", Origami⁶) are discussed and set aside: "no computational model has yet been developed for [hex pleating] due to a lack of practical demand" — [COrigami App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1). (That claim is now dated by B. Wong's Origami⁸ paper on hex-pleating packing, below.)
- "Elevation" appears only as the stick-figure elevation angle. BP-sense elevation (axial creases moved off levels 0/1) is not discussed — [COrigami §3.1, App. C](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Why BP Studio was rejected: its packing solver "operates on the discrete grid but solves a continuous relaxation", so "its solutions frequently contain Pythagorean stretches and cannot enforce contiguous tiling". A TreeMaker → BP Studio hybrid "encountered the same limitations" — [COrigami App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)

**Solving stage (§3.3, App. F)**
- Pleat construction is a 5-step filter of a dense orthogonal grid: (1) lay a global grid; (2) split at hinge/ridge intersections; (3) keep segments perpendicular to an intersecting hinge or through an X-vertex (four ridges); (4) re-add border-touching segments that share a vertex with a ridge and an established pleat; (5) fill remaining coordinates iteratively for full axial coverage — [COrigami App. F.1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Pleat M/V: group pleats into connected paths, sort by distance from the origin, BFS-alternate M/V between paths one grid unit apart — [COrigami App. F.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Ridge M/V: anchors at Y-vertices (two perpendicular ridges plus a pleat tip share one assignment) and at the border (neighbour heuristic, else Mountain). Propagation rules: X-vertex all equal; parallel adjacent ridges flipped; perpendicular ridges at an intersection equal — [COrigami App. F.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Hinges: priority-driven greedy state-space search. The score is the sum of per-vertex closeness to local flat-foldability, with ties broken by hinge length and symmetry. A symmetric hinge "traps" a pleat and returns it to the unassigned pool for later flipping. Hinges are partitioned into connected components ("each … corresponds to a specific joint in the uniaxial tree") and solved largest-first. Pruning rules: score degradation (S_current < S_parent), grid misalignment, and Maekawa-unsatisfiable vertices — [COrigami §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Local check: a crimp algorithm generalising Big-Little-Big (Alg. 1). Global check: a Python reimplementation of Akitaya–Demaine–Ku's facewise CSP (taco-taco / taco-tortilla), with components solved by DFS and propagation — [COrigami §2, App. D](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Collapsed base: "physically resembles a dense, flat-folded strip (or tube)" — [COrigami §3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1). Internal measurement on the released Ram sample: a solved base collapses to a strip one grid unit wide (Ram 1×16 units) — internal `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-06-corigami-tree-to-model-pipeline.md` (prior-art table)

**Shaping stage**
- Simple fold: the cut line is intersected with every flat-folded face; per-flap/river subsets can be shaped separately — [COrigami §3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Tree shaping: BFS from the root. The rotation axis must lie in the parent's paper plane, which determines R uniquely — [COrigami §3.6, App. G.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Narrowing via a "clip pattern algorithm": a template is propagated across the layers of a flat-folded flap by affine transforms along the folding path, with M/V swapped on Z-flips and lines clipped to face hulls. Templates are a base adapter plus a narrowing pleat, in symmetric and asymmetric variants, for flaps and rivers — [COrigami §3.5, App. G.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- RL: Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, single-step tool calls for all flaps. Reward −1 if invalid, else VLM score + 0.6·min(n/10, 1) diversity bonus. Batch 64, learning rate 1e-4, KL coefficient decayed from 1 to 1e-4 over 500 steps. Trained on the top-1000 first-stage models — [COrigami §3.6, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)

**Reported metrics**
- Funnel: 560,000 tree candidates → 113,276 valid stick figures (20.2%) → packing 55.3% pass → solving 79.2% → shaping 92.0% → 17,789 removed at simulation/VLM (7,490 low VLM score, 10,299 below tree-similarity 0.9) → 27,869 final (5.0% overall) — [COrigami §4.2, Fig. 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- "As structural complexity increases, a larger proportion of designs fail at the packing and solving stages" (Fig. 7, by stick and river count); "these greedy algorithms do not scale to more complex topologies" — [COrigami §4.2, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- TreeMaker automated baseline on 66,036 stick figures: 743 successes (1.1%); CP construction failed 74.4%; scale optimisation failed 10.6%; flat-foldability violation 13.2%; timeout 0.6%. The authors note this likely under-represents TreeMaker, because human-in-the-loop steps were skipped — [COrigami App. A, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Direct LLM generation of SVG CPs (Gemini fine-tuned on 400k TreeMaker-generated CPs, about 3.2B tokens): flat-foldability "plateaus near 60%" — [COrigami App. B](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- VLM judge (87 positive / 152 negative): Flash T=1, N=4 with Rubrics reaches acc 0.766 / F1 0.689; the "Double" tournament reaches acc 0.811 / AP 0.651 / F1 0.74; Pro scored below Flash — [COrigami §4.1, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Tree-similarity score: 1 − Procrustes d² on merged-chain joints — [COrigami App. C.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- No reported metric of paper efficiency: no grid sizes, no scale (flap length / paper side), no comparison to TreeMaker/BP Studio optimum — [COrigami §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1). Locally held "released samples" (18 folders) have grids 30, 34, 34, 38, 38, 40, 40, 42, 44, 44, 45, 46, 46, 48, 54, 58, 58, 60 (median 44); the grid is the folder-name suffix — local `~/Desktop/origami_samples/` listing (provenance unverified). One grid is odd (45, the hoopoe), consistent with E.1's even-rounding applying only to symmetric figures.

**Limitations named by the paper**
- Only box-pleating paradigms; "true transformational creativity … will ultimately stem from inventing entirely novel folding methods" — [COrigami §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- A limited shaping set (simple folds and narrowing) — [COrigami §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- No Pythagorean stretches or level shifters (efficiency) — [COrigami §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Greedy solver fails on dense trees; "Future work must address this by integrating more advanced machine learning techniques and robust exploration strategies" — [COrigami §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- The VLM sees zero-thickness renders, so high-scoring models "may still require significant physical post-processing and manual layer thinning". Bulk and paper creep are not modelled — [COrigami §3.8, App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- "Future work may explore other trade-offs between these objectives" (efficiency vs. success/recognisability) — [COrigami App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)

**Internal replication facts (this repo; Pleatwright)**
- Pleatwright on the 51 artifact trees: 51/51 complete; 39/51 globally certified (12 largest, grids 66–136, over the SAT size guard); grid range 10–136 — internal `docs/superpowers/evidence/2026-10-06-corigami-pipeline/README.md`, `results.md`. Median grid computed from `results.md`: 50.
- Deviation 1: rivers nest as full-width bands or L∞ offset bands and never snake around flaps, so "a long thin branch costs a full-width strip"; 3 of 109 branching edits exceed the grid cap — internal `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-06-corigami-tree-to-model-pipeline.md`. Branching edits gave a median grid 1.68× the unedited roll's — internal `docs/superpowers/evidence/2026-10-10-branching-packer/README.md`
- Packing ladder: mirror-symmetric rows → unmirrored rows → all lengths doubled. Example: dog_2's 11-unit river creates a hinge-parity conflict that symmetric rows cannot meet — internal spec and evidence README above.

### Inferences
- COrigami's efficiency is bounded below by E.1's heuristic G_init, but the reported pipeline returns the first grid at which its own heuristic packer succeeds. On top of that, the VLM may prefer a larger grid. So COrigami grids are an upper bound on what an orthogonal-only packer could reach, not a measured optimum.
- E.1 charges every flap after the four largest 2·l² (twice a quarter-circle-equivalent l² square). This is the main place a better lower bound could sharpen the starting grid. For star-like trees the true orthogonal minimum is a square-packing problem (see Q4).
- The 18 local samples (median grid 44) and Pleatwright's 51 rolls (median 50) cannot be compared: the trees differ.
- The tiling requirement (no empty cells) is what keeps COrigami away from Pythagorean stretches. A stretch-capable packer would need gap-free tilings with non-orthogonal stretch regions, or an inverse construction like BP Studio's (gadgets resolve overlaps; rivers are traced as contours).

### Gaps
- No public COrigami code repository was found. The provenance of the 18 local samples and of the "Visualiser" (`serve-dot-zipline…`) cited in the internal spec could not be verified from a public primary source.
- Packer details (traversal plan, wall-follower, scoring heuristic, maximum grid bound, ranking weights) are not published.
- The paper gives no grid-size distribution or scale statistics for its 27,869 models, so its efficiency cannot be quantified from the paper.
- The PDF was not compared against HTML v1 for differences. An external summary listed a "Jan 27th, 2026" date on the PDF, which conflicts with the 24 June 2026 submission and was not resolved.

## Q2. BP Studio: data model, when stretches are required, how GPS/GOPS gadgets are computed, optimizer, licence and module map

### Takeaway
BP Studio (MIT licence, TypeScript core plus a C++/WASM optimizer) models a design as a tree with rectangular flaps. Two flaps whose expanded AABBs collide in L∞ but are still Euclidean-separated form a "junction". The junction's overlap rectangle (ox, oy) must be resolved by a stretch pattern: a GOPS gadget found by factoring the half-area ox·oy/2 = u·v, with fallbacks to half-integral (Kamiya) and a "universal" GPS that always succeeds. Hinge contours are traced afterwards. Its optimizer is a three-stage pipeline: continuous SLSQP circle/rounded-rectangle packing with basin-hopping, then a CSP fit to the integer grid that tries grid sizes in increasing order, then large-neighbourhood search "tidying" that reduces contour vertices and uncovered junction area. It does not require full tiling or full CP flat-foldability; COrigami rejects it for exactly that reason.

**Core data flow (src/core)**
- The Core is a web worker per project. User inputs: tree structure, edge lengths, flap positions and sizes, and stretch-pattern choices. Task DAG: height → balance → structure → AABB → junction → {stretch → pattern, roughContour, invalidJunction} → traceContour → patternContour → graphics — [BPS/src/core/README.md](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/README.md); [BPS/src/core/design/tasks/README.md](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/tasks/README.md)
- Tasks are scheduled by priority: a task's priority is one more than the maximum of its dependents, and all dependents are re-queued after it runs (incremental recomputation while dragging) — [tasks/README.md](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/tasks/README.md)
- Junction detection: recursively compare children of each LCA by AABB intersection, using AABBs expanded by distance to the LCA. No global LCA table is needed — [BPS/src/core/design/tasks/junction.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/tasks/junction.ts)

**When a stretch is required (junction classification)**
- `createJunction(a, b, lca)`: with tree distance d, axis separations sx = max(l1 − r2, l2 − r1) and sy likewise. If sx ≤ 0, sy ≤ 0, or sx² + sy² < d², the junction is invalid (the flaps are too close in Euclidean distance; the layout is illegal). Otherwise it is a ValidJunction with overlap o = (d − sx, d − sy), plus quadrant direction and tip corner — [BPS/src/core/design/layout/junction/junction.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/junction/junction.ts)
- So a stretch is needed exactly when two flaps' L∞ separation is below the tree distance (expanded rectangles overlap by an ox × oy rectangle) while their Euclidean separation is at least d. That is the circle-packing condition applied to rectangle corners. This is how BP Studio recovers circle-packing-like efficiency on a grid (derived from the code above).
- Junction covering: a junction can be geometrically "covered" by another and then vanishes from the CP. "Practical covering" handles transitive chains (if A covers B and B covers C, but B is invalidated by A, C is not really covered) — [BPS/src/core/design/layout/junction/validJunction.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/junction/validJunction.ts)
- "Invasion": when a small flap, even after expanding to the LCA, is smaller than the overlap region in width or height, the overlap is carved out of the larger flap's corner in the rough contour. This is purely geometric — [validJunction.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/junction/validJunction.ts)

**Stretch / Repository / Configuration / Partition / Device / Gadget / Piece hierarchy**
- Outline: (1) group connected ValidJunctions into a `Stretch`; (2) each Stretch's `Repository` searches `Configuration`s (entry `configGenerator`); (3) each Configuration generates `Device`s for each `Partition`; (4) Devices are combined by a `Positioner`, and a successful positioning gives a valid `Pattern` — [layout/README.md](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/README.md)
- `Stretch` = "a group of connected ValidJunctions. Crease patterns on such a group will have to be determined together". Repositories are cached by structure signature during dragging — [BPS/src/core/design/layout/stretch.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/stretch.ts)
- `Device` = "the smallest movable unit in a Pattern". `Gadget` = "a component in a Device corresponding to a single JOverlap. It always ha[s] four JAnchors" — [BPS/.../pattern/device.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/pattern/device.ts); [gadget.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/pattern/gadget.ts)
- `JPiece` = {ox, oy: overlap rectangle (OR) width/height; u, v: margin rectangle (MR) width/height; detours; shift (for non-integral solutions or complex joins)}. Anchors are gadget corners that connect to outer ridges and carry optional slack — [BPS/src/shared/json/pattern.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/shared/json/pattern.ts)
- Join strategies between overlaps: simple, perfect, base join, standard join (and others in the enum) — [BPS/src/shared/json/enum.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/shared/json/enum.ts)

**GOPS computation (core/math/gops.ts)**
- Integral GOPS for overlap (ox, oy): no solution if both are odd. Otherwise enumerate factorisations of the half-area ox·oy/2 = u·v, starting from u = ⌊√(halfArea)⌋ and searching down ("This will prioritize the most efficient gadget"); both (u, v) and (v, u) are kept. Each candidate is stored with span sx = u + v + oy, and `generate(ox, oy, sx_max)` yields those within a span bound — [BPS/src/core/math/gops.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/math/gops.ts)
- Rank (lower is better) = max(reduced numerator of (oy+v)/oy, of (ox+u)/ox). In effect this is the complexity of the slopes the gadget's creases need; ties between (u, v) and (v, u) are ordered by rank — [gops.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/math/gops.ts)
- Half-integral "Kamiya" gadgets: for odd ox and odd oy, double the overlap, run GOPS, keep only rank ≤ 3 ("The rank of Kamiya patterns is 3"), halve, and build a two-piece gadget with a slope-3 detour shift — [BPS/src/core/math/kamiya.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/math/kamiya.ts)
- "Universal GPS strategy. Guaranteed to find a pattern in a single overlap": scale the overlap by d = 2, 4, 6, … until a GOPS with integral v after shrinking exists, then build a two-piece gadget (piece and its transpose) and add the slack needed to reach the next integer — [BPS/.../generators/universalGPS.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/generators/universalGPS.ts)
- Strategy order for a single junction: single integral gadget → "double relay" (split the overlap into two pieces along the shorter side, skipping odd-area overlaps) → half-integral → universal. `GeneratorUtil.$first` takes the first strategy that yields — [BPS/.../generators/singleConfigGenerator.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/generators/singleConfigGenerator.ts)
- Multiple junctions: `generalConfigGenerator` checks preconditions, then enumerates combinations by increasing total rank (rank 0 … maxRank) and searches each — [BPS/.../generators/generalConfigGenerator.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/generators/generalConfigGenerator.ts). Span lower bounds (gadget width spans plus relay slacks along chains) are checked as soon as the last partition on a chain has a device chosen, and slacks are cached per device pair. The comment records this took "a search from seconds down to ~100ms" — [BPS/.../generators/spanBound.ts](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/generators/spanBound.ts)
- Other generator modules (not read line by line): `deviceGenerator.ts`, `patternGenerator.ts`, `filters.ts`, `searchUtils/{rank, splitJoin, relay, bridge, plan}.ts`, `joiner/{fanJoin, joiner, logic/standardJoinLogic, simpleJoinLogic, baseJoinLogic}.ts`, `pattern/positioners/{jointPositioner, singleJunctionPositioner}.ts` — [BPS/src/core/design/layout/](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/tree/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout)

**Hinge-contour tracing (after patterns)**
- (1) Rough contours: a flap's is its AABB; a river's is the union of its children's expanded rough contours, computed with fast axis-aligned unions (works "in most (say over 90%) of the scenarios"). (2) Where a union hides a needed quadrant corner, keep that component as a "raw contour". (3) Split into HingeSegments with start/end points. (4) Trace to PatternContours. (5) Combine with the rough contour, using general polygon union if raw contours were used. Simply reflecting hinges about stretch ridges "only works when the entire layout is valid" — [layout/README.md](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/core/design/layout/README.md)
- Internal reading of the same code: a hub's contour is the union of its children's contours expanded by edge length; the river is that contour minus the children; "Every right-angle river corner inside the sheet must have an inward 45° ridge" (`core/design/health/riverCorner.ts`) — internal `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-branching-hub-packer-design.md`

**Optimizer (src/client/plugins/optimizer, C++ → WASM via Emscripten, NLopt)**
- Ported from Python/SciPy/Pyodide to C++: up to 10× faster, package 69 MB → under 1 MB, 10× less memory. The SciPy idea came from Brandon Wong — [BPS/.../optimizer/src/README.md](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/client/plugins/optimizer/src/README.md)
- Stage 1, pre-solve (continuous): minimise sheet size subject to pairwise flap distances ≥ structural distances ("the classical circle-river packing problem … implemented in … TreeMaker"), solved by SLSQP in NLopt. Start either from the current layout (optionally with basin-hopping "variations") or from random tree-aware layouts: simplify big branches into single flaps of comparable area, randomise, then refine recursively. Basin-hopping is always on in random mode — [optimizer README](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/client/plugins/optimizer/src/README.md)
- Constraint forms: `CircleConstraint` (x_i − x_j)² + (y_i − y_j)² ≥ (m·d)² for point flaps; `RoundedConstraint` for flaps with width/height uses signed interval distances dx, dy between scaled rectangles, with (m·d)² − dx² − dy² ≤ 0 (a Euclidean rounded-rectangle separation) — [constraint/circleConstraint.cpp](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/client/plugins/optimizer/src/constraint/circleConstraint.cpp); [roundedConstraint.cpp](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/client/plugins/optimizer/src/constraint/roundedConstraint.cpp)
- Stage 2, fitting (integer): the old method was NLP diving with no backtracking. The current method is a binary CSP. Start one sheet size below ⌈continuous⌉ ("occasionally possible to beat the continuous solution on grids"). Each flap's candidates are the floor/ceil window, enlarged up to 8×8. Pairs that cannot violate are dropped. Solve with arc consistency plus backtracking (MRV variable order, closeness value order, node limit), verify in exact integers, else enlarge the window and then the sheet. "a few milliseconds even for designs with a hundred flaps", sheets "equal to or smaller than those produced by NLP diving" — [optimizer README](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/client/plugins/optimizer/src/README.md); [branching/csp.h](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/client/plugins/optimizer/src/branching/csp.h)
- Stage 3, tidying (fixed sheet size): the lexicographic objective is (1) total rough-contour vertex count of flaps and rivers (fewer means straighter rivers and a simpler CP), then (2) total area of uncovered junctions (sliding toward axis alignment shrinks junctions, "push[ing] the flaps into neat rows and columns"). The search is Large Neighbourhood Search: seeds are uncovered junctions (largest first), then misaligned adjacent pairs. Each group is the seed pair plus up to 4 interacting flaps; it is re-optimised exactly with CSP machinery and branch-and-bound on a pairwise proxy (lines occupied by vertices of the LCA-expanded pair union, plus covering-weighted junction area). Accept only if the true objective improves. Then single-flap moves and pairwise swaps with neighbourhood repair; optional "partial moves" (proper subsets of the proxy optimum). Several runs with and without covering weights and partial moves cost about 1.8× the first two runs alone — [optimizer README](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/client/plugins/optimizer/src/README.md)
- BP Studio also includes a TreeMaker 5 file parser (`plugins/treeMaker`) and a CP export plugin (`plugins/cp`) — [BPS/src/client/plugins/](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/tree/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/client/plugins)

**Practitioner gadget theory (Abrashi, a COrigami co-author)**
- Pythagorean stretch: needed when "two adjacent flap polygons overlap while their inscribed circles do not". Integer triples (3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25) keep corners on grid. The non-flexible feature is a 135° angle at the hypotenuse corners. A parallelogram stretch exists iff at least one overlap side is even; otherwise use the "universal" stretch (3×1 example). Grid 135° constructions include 1:2–1:3 and 2:5–3:7. Box-pleated models can be "almost 40%" smaller than circle-packed in extreme cases [as written in source], and the stretch brings BP to "almost the same optimality level" — [Abrashi, "Pythagorean stretch explained"](https://abrashiorigami.com/pythagorean-stretch/) (the 40% direction appears inverted relative to standard theory; see Inferences)
- Level shifters: change the elevation of axial creases (default valley at 0, mountain at 1), widening every flap the elevated crease passes through until another shifter lowers it. They are implemented on ridge creases. Types on 45° ridges need no dummy element and lose no paper (asymmetric version 2 units per level, mirror 3, symmetric about 2.5). Types on horizontal/vertical ridges need a host and always lose paper (the hidden version loses 4 units and creates 5 layers per elevation step) — [Abrashi, "Level shifters explained"](https://abrashiorigami.com/level-shifters/)

### Inferences
- The algorithmic core is: a stretch is a local repair of an L∞ violation that is Euclidean-feasible, and its existence and cost are number-theoretic (factorisations of ox·oy/2). An implementer can bolt this onto an orthogonal packer as a post-pass: pack with Euclidean (rounded-rectangle) constraints on the integer grid, then resolve each connected junction group with GOPS → relay → Kamiya → universal. This is BP Studio's design.
- BP Studio's notion of validity is "every junction resolved by a stretch pattern, rivers traced"; it does not require gap-free tiling or emit a fully assigned, globally certified CP. "Fully flat-foldable CP export" was still on the 2021 roadmap. That fits COrigami's complaint and means an automated pipeline would still need its own M/V solving and certification on top of BP Studio's geometry.
- The optimizer's three-stage design (continuous relaxation → windowed CSP → LNS tidy) transfers directly to any grid packer. The tidy objective (contour vertices, then uncovered junction area) is a usable proxy for CP simplicity, and so for foldability and recognisability.
- Abrashi's "almost 40%" sentence, as summarised, says box-pleated models can be smaller than circle-packed ones. The standard result is the reverse: L∞ separation needs at least as much paper as Euclidean separation, up to a √2 factor in distance for diagonal pairs (1 − 1/√2 ≈ 29% scale). Read the source directly before citing that figure.

### Gaps
- The full GOPS construction (Lang & Tsai Origami⁷; Tsai Origami⁸) is paywalled and was not read. The factorisation rule is taken from the BP Studio code.
- `deviceGenerator`, the positioners and the joiner logic were not analysed in detail. The exact rules for multi-flap ("fan", "split/relay") joins are summarised from file names and comments only.
- BP Studio's user manual pages (definitions in the UI sense) were not fetched; the notes page held only a bibliography.

## Q3. Other automated or semi-automated BP tools (TreeMaker, Pleatsmith, ORIPA/Oriedita, flat-folder, ML)

### Takeaway
No public tool besides COrigami (code unreleased) automates tree → gap-free BP tiling → assigned, flat-foldable base. TreeMaker optimises circle/river packing (scale m) and builds universal-molecule CPs, but its hard topologies need manual steps; automated, it succeeded on 1.1% of COrigami's stick figures. Pleatsmith (benwalley) generates CPs from user-placed BP layouts with no packing automation and no stretch or level-shifter gadgets. ORIPA, Origami Simulator and Akitaya–Demaine–Ku's flat-folder are analysis tools, not packers. ML attempts at direct CP generation plateau around 60% flat-foldable.

### Cited Findings
- TreeMaker NLCO: maximise the scale m over leaf vertices {v_i} subject to |v_i − v_j| ≥ m·l_ij and v_i ∈ P. "The scale is a measure of the efficiency of the folded base"; the active paths are the active constraints — [Lang & Demaine, OSME 2006, pp. 191–192 (PDF)](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/TreeMaker_OSME2006/paper.pdf)
- Lang & Demaine give a facet-ordering digraph algorithm that, with the NLCO, "provides a complete computation algorithm for the crease pattern, crease assignment, and stacking order for an arbitrary uniaxial origami base". It is implemented in TreeMaker (optimisation, construction and assignment total about 27,500 lines of code). The proof is deferred: "we have not proven that the ROG satisfies the Justin conditions", though it was tested on pathological cases — [Lang & Demaine 2006 (PDF)](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/TreeMaker_OSME2006/paper.pdf)
- Manual TreeMaker steps COrigami could not automate: symmetry pairing about a line; fracturing high-order polygons with stubs; forcing leaves to edges; repositioning nodes to escape local optima — [COrigami App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1). Automated TreeMaker success: 1.1% (743/66,036) — [COrigami Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Internal: this repo's archived Lang Ch. 13 BP compiler (`box_pleat/`) emitted 7/51 on the artifact trees (31 hit a 300 s cap) — internal `docs/superpowers/evidence/2026-10-06-corigami-pipeline/README.md`
- Pleatsmith (`benwalley/Box-pleating-generator`): the user places flap tips, sizes and rivers. It infers the tree from L∞ distances, builds an ownership field, generates hinges, ridges, axial and contour creases, assigns M/V "where it can" (hinges are always left unassigned), and runs local Maekawa/Kawasaki checks. It does not automate packing. It does not generate Pythagorean/GOPS stretches, level shifters, half-unit structures or meandering rivers; these appear as warnings. 9 commits; no licence shown — [GitHub benwalley/Box-pleating-generator](https://github.com/benwalley/Box-pleating-generator)
- ORIPA (Mitani 2005): a CP editor with folded-shape estimation. Origami Simulator (Ghassaei, Demaine, Gershenfeld 2018): GPU mass-spring simulation. COrigami found the latter "tends to accumulate relatively high strain errors" in automation and wrote its own geometric folder — [COrigami §3.7, App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Facewise global flat-foldability: Akitaya, Demaine, Ku, "Computing Flat-Folded States" (Origami⁸, OSME 2024) turns layer ordering into a finite CSP over overlapping face pairs (taco-taco, taco-tortilla, …) — [Demaine paper page](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/FlatFolder_OSME2024/); COrigami describes it as polynomial O(n³) "to compute valid layer-ordering" — [COrigami App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1) (the O(n³) claim is COrigami's and was not checked against the Akitaya et al. text; general flat-foldability is NP-hard, so it must refer to a restricted sub-step)
- ML: Gemini fine-tuned on 400k synthetic CPs plateaus near 60% flat-foldability — [COrigami App. B](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1). OrigamiSpace (Xu et al. 2025) and OrigamiBench (Agarwal et al. 2026, arXiv 2603.13856) document MLLM spatial-reasoning deficits — [COrigami §1, App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- Hex pleating: B. Wong, "New Techniques in Hex Pleating for Representational Origami Design", Origami⁸ vol. IV pp. 21–38 (2025), covers packing uniaxial trees on a hex grid, redistributing thickness, and level shifters. Wong's slides: hex-pleating level shifters "are more efficient, and can be easily 'built in' by using ridge bevels" — [Springer ch. 2](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-6711-6_2); [Wong slides PDF](https://web.mit.edu/wongb/www/origami/engineering/files/hp%20slides.pdf). Also cited there: Wong & Demaine (2024), "Algorithmic transitions between parallel pleats", 8OSME (not read).

### Inferences
- The tooling landscape divides cleanly. Efficiency-first interactive tools (TreeMaker, BP Studio) leave tiling, assignment and certification to the human. Automation-first COrigami leaves efficiency on the table. A Pleatwright efficiency effort would sit in the gap, for example orthogonal tiling plus BP Studio-style junction gadgets as an optional rung on the packing ladder.
- Since COrigami co-authors include Lang, Wong, Abrashi and Kosmulski, practitioner explainers by these authors (e.g. Abrashi's) are probably the closest public description of the gadget vocabulary COrigami's team considered and deferred.

### Gaps
- No other 2020–2026 paper on automated BP packing optimisation was found. Searches surfaced only COrigami, BP Studio's optimizer, Tsai's 8OSME GOPS paper, and Wong's hex-pleating paper. Oriedita BP helpers and other community tools were not investigated (Tsai contributes to Oriedita per [BP Studio notes](https://bp-studio.github.io/notes.html)).
- The requested Ku & Demaine paper on "folding flat crease patterns with uniaxial hinge structures" could not be identified. The closest match found is Ku & Demaine, "Folding Flat Crease Patterns with Thick Materials" (J. Mechanisms and Robotics; arXiv 1601.05747) — [PDF](http://jasonku.mit.edu/pdf/THICKFOLDING_JMR.pdf). It is about thickness, not uniaxial hinge structures, and was not read in full. The title may be misremembered or unpublished.

## Q4. Theoretical efficiency comparisons and complexity/hardness results that constrain automation

### Takeaway
Every design-side problem relevant here is NP-hard in general. Circle/river (tree-method) optimisation is NP-hard on square paper; packing squares into a square (the star-tree case of orthogonal BP packing) is NP-complete; flat-foldability of box-pleated CPs is NP-complete, assigned (≤25 layers) or unassigned (≤9 layers). Crease assignment for uniaxial bases produced by the tree method is tractable in practice (Lang–Demaine ordering graph, proof deferred). The 22.5° system has a closed-form constructible-coordinate set. So automation must lean on heuristics, decomposition and search caps, as COrigami's greedy partitioned solver and BP Studio's windowed CSP/LNS both do. Exact optimal-efficiency packing is not a realistic target.

### Cited Findings
- **Box pleating is hard**: Akitaya, Cheung, Demaine, Horiyama, Hull, Ku, Tachi, Uehara, LNCS 9943 (JCDCGG 2015), pp. 167–179. "Unassigned-Flat-Foldability and Assigned-Flat-Foldability are NP-complete, even for box pleated crease patterns containing no more than 9 and 25 layers respectively." Box pleating here means vertices on the integer lattice with creases at multiples of 45°. Assigned-k-Layer-Flat-Foldability is NP-complete for k ≥ 25. They found an error in Bern–Hayes' unassigned crossover gadget. Open: assigned flat-foldability for an m×n map, and "orthogonal" (axis-only) CPs; for orthogonal CPs, unassigned is poly and assigned is open — [LNCS PDF](https://scripts.mit.edu/~jasonku/pdf/BOXPLEATINGHARD_LNCS2016.pdf); [Demaine page](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/BoxPleatingHard_JCDCGG2015full/)
- **Circle packing for origami design is hard**: Demaine, Fekete, Lang (Origami⁵, 2010, pp. 609–626; arXiv 1008.1224). "The general tree method optimization problem is NP-hard", by reduction from 3-Partition, with theorems for triangular, rectangular and square paper (Thm 7.1: circle/river design for square paper is NP-hard). Corollary: deciding whether given circles pack into a square is NP-hard. Positive result: circles of total area 1 fit in a square of side 4/√π ≈ 2.2567 — [arXiv 1008.1224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.1224); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1224)
- The same paper: once optimisation is done, "the process of constructing the creases and assigning them is definitely polynomial in complexity" for the tree method. It notes that Leung et al. proved square packing (deciding whether given squares pack into a unit square) NP-complete via 3-Partition — [arXiv 1008.1224 PDF, §1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1224)
- **Bern & Hayes (SODA 1996)**: general crease assignment is NP-complete, and so is stacking order given a full assignment — as restated in [Demaine–Fekete–Lang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1224) and [Box Pleating is Hard](https://scripts.mit.edu/~jasonku/pdf/BOXPLEATINGHARD_LNCS2016.pdf)
- **Lang & Demaine (Origami⁴, 2006)**: "Fortunately, determination of the stacking order and crease assignment in uniaxial bases is not NP-complete, as we will show". Their method builds a facet-ordering digraph; the proof that it satisfies Justin's conditions is deferred — [PDF](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/TreeMaker_OSME2006/paper.pdf)
- **22.5° system**: Tachi & Demaine, "Degenerative Coordinates in 22.5° Grid System" (Origami⁵/OSME 2010). Constructible points are exactly those with coordinates (m + n√2)/2^ℓ, and the construction count is linear in bit size. The authors credit the degeneracy (many coincidences) with keeping complex 22.5° CPs organised. Generalised to π/n by Butler, Demaine, Graham, Tachi (IJCGA 2013) — [paper PDF](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/AngularGrid_OSME2010/paper.pdf); [Demaine page](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/AngularGrid_Origami5/) (summarised via search; PDF not read in full)
- **Finite-propagation grids**: Lang & Alperin (2014, Origami⁶ I pp. 305–318) show a countable infinity of angle-constrained grids with finite crease propagation; COrigami judges most "impractical" — [COrigami App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- **Infinite bouncing**: continuous routing with restricted angles can still yield non-terminating crease propagation, one motivation for grids — [COrigami §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- **Universality of BP**: an N×N box-pleat grid suffices to fold any polycube of O(N) cubes (universal hinge patterns; Benbernou, Demaine, Demaine, Ovadya, Origami⁵ 2010) — cited in [Box Pleating is Hard refs](https://scripts.mit.edu/~jasonku/pdf/BOXPLEATINGHARD_LNCS2016.pdf); search summary of [arXiv 1611.03187](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.03187)
- **COrigami's own framing**: "intractability that persists even under restricted box-pleating grids due to cascading parity constraints and exponential branching factors", yet "simple, priority-driven greedy algorithms were surprisingly efficient for fairly complex models" thanks to hierarchical partitions — [COrigami §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26299v1)
- **Efficiency comparison (practitioner)**: Pythagorean stretches let BP "reach almost the same optimality level" as circle packing — [Abrashi](https://abrashiorigami.com/pythagorean-stretch/). Level shifters on 45° ridges cost no paper; those on axis-parallel ridges always lose some — [Abrashi](https://abrashiorigami.com/level-shifters/)
- **Efficiency in BP Studio's fitter**: the integer-grid sheet can occasionally be smaller than ⌈continuous optimum⌉, so the search starts one size below — [BPS optimizer README](https://github.com/MuTsunTsai/box-pleating-studio/blob/ae73ae7df05a33e4742bd1c7ae3f808efb923a4d/src/client/plugins/optimizer/src/README.md)

### Inferences
- Plain uniaxial BP (flap squares, L∞ separation ≥ tree distance) is the L∞ analogue of the tree-method NLCO. For a star tree it reduces to packing squares of side 2·l_i into the sheet, which is NP-complete by Leung et al. So optimal orthogonal BP packing is NP-hard at least for stars. This is my reduction sketch, not a published theorem found in these sources.
- Efficiency ordering, for the same tree: circle/river (Euclidean) ≥ BP + Pythagorean stretches (Euclidean-feasible on the grid, with integrality losses) ≥ orthogonal BP with level shifters/elevation (L∞, but wider flaps without extra rows) ≥ COrigami-style orthogonal tiling ≥ string-of-beads stacking. Distance-wise, L∞ separation costs up to √2× the Euclidean for diagonal flap pairs. The worst case is about 29% less flap length per unit paper for that pair; actual packing losses depend on topology. No measured comparison on a common tree set was found.
- The ≤9 and ≤25 layer bounds say hardness holds at layer counts COrigami bases routinely exceed. Pleatwright's CERTIFIED vs. OVERSIZE+LOCAL_SAT split (12/51 over the SAT guard) is an expected consequence, not a bug.
- 22.5° buys finer angular freedom (fewer wasted rows for some flap arrangements) at the cost of irrational-but-constructible coordinates ((m + n√2)/2^ℓ). That is compatible with automation (exact arithmetic in ℤ[√2]) but outside COrigami's integer-grid tiling model.

### Gaps
- No published theorem was found that states NP-hardness of uniaxial box-pleat packing directly (only circle/river and generic square packing).
- No source gives quantitative efficiency comparisons (e.g. scale ratios) between circle packing, BP with/without stretches, 22.5° and hybrids on a common benchmark.
- The Akitaya–Demaine–Ku Origami⁸ paper's exact complexity claims (COrigami's "O(n³)") were not verified from its text.
- Lang's *Origami Design Secrets* (2nd ed., 2011) chapters on box pleating, uniaxial box pleating, polygon packing and level shifters are the canonical primary sources but were not accessible. Gadget costs are cited from practitioner explainers instead.
